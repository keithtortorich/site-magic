#  Company Strength & Positioning Assessment

## Overview

This protocol defines **how the skill assesses company strengths, competitive gaps, and market positioning** using live web research. It's designed to be systematic, repeatable, and grounded in actual customer sentiment \+ competitive intelligence.

---

## Part 1: Company Strengths Assessment

### 1.1 Review Site Analysis

**Platform:** Google Reviews, Yelp, Angie's List, Home Advisor, service-specific directories

**Search queries:**

- `[business name] reviews`  
- `[service type] [city/zip] reviews`  
- `[business name] + [competing business] comparison`

**What to extract:**

- **Rating average** (4.5/5? 3.2/5? How does it compare to competitors?)  
- **Review volume** (100 reviews vs. 10? Signal of scale and trust)  
- **Recurring themes in positive reviews:**  
  - Speed (same-day, emergency response)  
  - Quality (professional, thorough, good workmanship)  
  - Reliability (showed up on time, finished on schedule)  
  - Customer service (friendly, respectful, communicative)  
  - Pricing (fair, transparent, no surprises)  
  - Safety/credentials (licensed, insured, certified)

**Scoring logic:**

Strength identified if:

\- Mentioned in ≥3 reviews (real pattern, not anomaly)

\- Rated as primary reason for choosing business (in review summary)

\- Differentiated from competitors (they don't mention this)

**Output example:**

REVIEW SENTIMENT ANALYSIS

Avg rating: 4.7/5 (67 reviews, Google \+ Yelp)

Top strength: Speed (mentioned 28% of reviews)

  \> "Showed up in 3 hours, emergency fixed same day"

  \> "Fastest response of any plumber I've tried"

  \> Competitor avg: 24-hour response, positioning as "quick"

  \> DIFFERENTIATION OPPORTUNITY: Emphasize "same-day" or "2-hour response guarantee"

Second strength: Pricing transparency (mentioned 18% of reviews)

  \> "No hidden fees, quoted price is what you pay"

  \> Competitor messaging: generic "competitive pricing"

  \> DIFFERENTIATION OPPORTUNITY: "Upfront pricing" or "no surprise charges"

Weakness/complaint: Communication delays (mentioned 8% of reviews)

  \> "Took 2 days to hear back about scheduling"

  \> OPPORTUNITY: Emphasize "respond within 2 hours" in ads

### 1.2 Website Analysis

**What to extract:**

- Current positioning statement (homepage headline)  
- Service packages offered \+ pricing (transparent or opaque?)  
- Credentials displayed (license, insurance, certifications, awards)  
- Trust signals (testimonials, case studies, years in business, team photos)  
- Call-to-action style (urgent vs. consultative vs. transactional)

**Scoring logic:**

Weakness if:

\- No pricing visible (customers hate this in service industry)

\- No visible credentials/licensing

\- No recent testimonials or proof of active business

\- CTA is vague ("contact us") vs. action-oriented ("schedule free inspection")

Strength if:

\- Transparent pricing \+ service packages

\- Clear credentials (license \#, certifications, awards)

\- Recent testimonials with specific wins (dates, problems solved)

\- Action-oriented CTA \+ frictionless booking

**Output example:**

WEBSITE AUDIT

Positioning: "Trusted HVAC experts since 1998"

  \> Generic, common in industry

Pricing visibility: NONE (red flag)

  \> Competitors: 2 show price ranges, 1 hides like this company

  \> OPPORTUNITY: "Upfront pricing" could be differentiator

Credentials: License \#45XXX, 25 years, some testimonials

  \> Solid trust signals, but not unique

CTA: "Contact us for a free quote" (phone button prominent)

  \> Action-oriented, but requires phone call

  \> Competitors: 1 offers online booking, 1 has "instant quote" tool

  \> OPPORTUNITY: "Schedule online in 60 seconds" could reduce friction

### 1.3 Social Media Presence

**Platforms:** Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn (if B2B), Google Business Profile

**What to extract:**

- Activity level (regular posts? abandoned?)  
- Content type (project showcase, educational tips, customer spotlights, promotions)  
- Engagement (likes, comments, shares per post)  
- Tone (professional, friendly, casual, sales-y)  
- Customer response (do comments show satisfaction?)

**Scoring logic:**

Strength if:

\- Regular posting (≥2x/week)

\- Mix of project showcase \+ educational \+ customer spotlight

\- High engagement \+ positive customer comments

\- Professional but approachable tone

Weakness if:

\- Abandoned accounts (last post 3+ months ago)

\- Only promotional content (constant "call us" messages)

\- Minimal engagement (5 likes per post)

\- Mismatch between brand promise and social reality (claims "professional" but posts are sloppy)

**Output example:**

SOCIAL MEDIA AUDIT

Facebook: 1,200 followers, active, \~3 posts/week

  \> Content: 40% project showcase, 30% safety tips, 20% customer spotlights, 10% promotions

  \> Engagement: avg 50 likes, 8 comments per post

  \> Tone: professional, friendly, customer-focused

  \> STRENGTH: Consistent engagement \+ educational value

Instagram: 300 followers, sparse (\~1 post/month)

  \> Content: mostly project photos, no captions or storytelling

  \> Engagement: avg 20 likes

  \> OPPORTUNITY: Activate Instagram (competitor is much more active here)

### 2.2 Messaging & Positioning Themes Analysis

**Common themes observed across competitors:**

MESSAGING PATTERNS (Frequency \= how many competitors claim this)

"Licensed and insured"  -  Frequency: 5/8

## Part 3: Customer Pain Point & Intent Research

### 3.1 Customer Language & Pain Points

**Search queries on forums/Reddit:**

- `[service type] [city] help` (e.g., "plumber help chicago")  
- `r/[city]` \+ service type (e.g., "best plumber in chicago")  
- `[service type] problems` (e.g., "plumber problems"  -  what frustrates customers?)

**Customer pain points to extract:**

Common reasons for purchase urgency:

\- Emergency (burst pipe, HVAC failure)

\- Maintenance (seasonal, preventive)

\- Home sale/purchase contingency (inspection failure)

Common frustrations with service providers:

\- Long wait times

\- Hidden fees / surprise pricing

\- Unprofessional behavior

\- No-shows / rescheduling

\- Poor communication (hard to reach, slow callbacks)

What customers value:

\- Speed of response

\- Upfront, transparent pricing

\- Professional but friendly demeanor

\- Respect for home (drop cloths, cleanup)

\- Warranty / guarantees on work

**Output:**

CUSTOMER INTENT & PAIN POINTS

Primary trigger for purchase: Emergency (70% of searches are "emergency \[service\]")

  \> Emotional state: Anxious, urgent

  \> Messaging opportunity: "Emergency available 24/7", "respond in 2 hours"

Secondary trigger: Maintenance (25%)

  \> Emotional state: Preventive, budget-conscious

  \> Messaging opportunity: "Maintenance packages", "prevent expensive repairs"

Top 3 frustrations:

1\. "Can't reach anyone fast"  -  Messaging: "Respond within 2 hours"

2\. "Surprised by bill"  -  Messaging: "Upfront pricing, no surprises"

3\. "They didn't show up"  -  Messaging: "Guaranteed appointment time or $50 credit"

## Part 4: Positioning Recommendation Logic

### 4.1 The Venn Diagram

Position at intersection of:

1. **Company strength** (what they're actually good at, per reviews \+ analysis)  
2. **Competitor white space** (what no one's claiming)  
3. **Customer need** (what drives purchase intent and frustration)

**Example:**

COMPANY STRENGTH: High speed (28% of reviews mention "same-day" or "fast")

COMPETITOR WHITE SPACE: No one credibly claims "2-hour response guarantee"

CUSTOMER NEED: "Can't reach anyone fast" is \#1 complaint

RECOMMENDED POSITIONING: "2-hour response guarantee, always"

  \> Owned by company strength (they actually deliver this)

  \> Differentiated (competitors say "fast" but no guarantee)

  \> Solves customer pain (directly addresses \#1 frustration)

### 4.2 Positioning Statement Template

**Primary positioning:** \[1-2 sentences\]

- Should own one unique, defensible claim  
- Should connect to customer pain or company strength  
- Should be difficult for competitors to copy (if based on ops, not just marketing claim)

**Example:**

- ❌ "We're the best electrician in town" (everyone says this)  
- ✅ "Emergency electrician, 2-hour response time, guaranteed" (specific, ownable, credible)

**Secondary positioning:** \[optional, for segment differentiation\]

- If they serve both homeowners \+ contractors, might have different messaging per segment  
- Or if they do both emergency \+ maintenance, might emphasize different benefits

**Example:**

- Primary: Homeowners → "Emergency available, transparent pricing"  
- Secondary: Contractors → "Fast turnaround on bulk work, volume pricing"

### 4.3 Messaging Pillars (3 supporting claims)

Each pillar should reinforce the primary positioning:

**Example for "2-hour response, transparent pricing" positioning:**

- Pillar 1: "Licensed, insured, certified" (trust \+ credential)  
- Pillar 2: "Upfront quote before work starts" (transparency)  
- Pillar 3: "Warranty on all work" (confidence \+ customer protection)

**NOT:**

- Pillar 1: "We've been around 20 years" (company-centric, not customer-benefit)  
- Pillar 2: "Friendly staff" (expected, not differentiated)  
- Pillar 3: "We love what we do" (claims without proof)

## Part 5: Quality Assurance Checklist

Before finalizing research output, verify:

- [ ] ≥3 competitive sources checked (Google, Yelp, own website, social media)  
- [ ] Positioning claim is **defendable** (not just marketing fluff)  
      - [ ] Based on actual customer feedback (reviews) or company ops, not invented  
      - [ ] Different from ≥70% of competitors  
      - [ ] Solves real customer pain point  
- [ ] White space opportunities are **realistic**  
      - [ ] Not already claimed by stronger competitor  
      - [ ] Ownable (company can actually deliver)  
- [ ] Customer language is **derived from actual sources**  
      - [ ] Reviews, forums, Google search terms  
      - [ ] Not generic assumptions

❌ **Reject if:**

- All claims are generic ("fast, reliable, professional")  
- White space is not actually white (competitors own it too)  
- Company strength contradicts positioning claim  
- No customer validation for the pain point

✅ **Accept if:**

- ≥2 of 3 pillars have specific, customer-quoted validation  
- Positioning is differentiated in the competitive set  
- Customer pain point is real \+ urgent
